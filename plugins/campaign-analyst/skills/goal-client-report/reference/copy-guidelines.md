# GOAL report copy guidelines

Reports deliver information and present it visually. The objective and any narrative are carried by the visuals and by the Account Manager's live conversation, never by report copy.

**Core principle:** accurate metrics plus a clean visual. No narrative, no opinion, no filler. If the data cannot make the point visually, stop and flag it to the Account Manager with alternative visual options.

If `~/GOAL/My Accounts/GOAL Report Copy Guidelines.md` exists on the user's computer and differs from this file, that file wins.

## Voice and framing
- Never address the client by name in body copy. Identity lives in the sidebar and footer only ("Prepared for [Contact Name]").
- No first-name greeting in the executive summary.
- GOAL account actions use "we": "We increased the daily budget from $50 to $95."
- Never reference the client's emotions, expectations, or shortfalls ("you expected more volume", "the first day did not deliver", "that is a fair frustration").
- Concise, professional, dry. No flowery, cheeky, or descriptive language. No metaphors. No rhetorical constructions ("every update is aimed at that number").
- No em dashes. Use a comma, period, colon, or "|".

## Section sub-headers
Section and recommendation headings use Title Case ("Key Metrics", "Respond to New Leads Within 5 Minutes").

Scope and dates only: "June 1–30", "Last 30 days", "Next review period". Never an interpretive claim ("every stage after the impression is strong" is jargon that implies an unstated problem).

## Chart and figure notes
- State the metrics, nothing else: "Shoppers clicked the ad on 4.6% of impressions (82 of 1,782). 72% of clicks became a lead (59)."
- No commentary on what a metric means ("the ad is doing its job", "the constraint is visibility"). Praise weakens a strong number; conclusions belong in the conversation.
- Plain-language labels for non-experts, with the term as a sublabel: "Your ad shown" over "impressions".
- Line-table notes: the line's metrics plus any update made. No interpretation.

## Recommendations
Every report does two things:
1. **Current status over a reasonable window.** Pick a window long enough that single-day judgments look premature. Let the data show this; never state it.
2. **The updates being made and the data behind them.** Each item has a plain imperative heading, one or two factual sentences, and a "Metric behind it:" line with the specific number that motivated it. Frame items as opportunities toward the agency's objective, not as a list of what was broken. Tag each item `GOAL` (we own it) or `Agency` (the agency owns it). Mark completed GOAL actions in past tense.

The section is titled **Recommendations**. Never "What we're doing for you" or "Your growth plan".

## Metrics
- **CPA (cost per policy) is the headline efficiency metric.** Then contact rate, quote rate, and stage conversion.
- **Never show cost per lead (CPL).** GOAL runs ad campaigns; it does not sell leads.
- Every conversion rate uses Leads as the denominator (or the stage above it inside the funnel), never Clicks.
- Never invent, estimate, or project numbers. If a metric is missing from the exports, omit the card or row.
- No projections, estimated results, or close-rate promises.

## Hours and the schedule
- Hours are always the agent's local time, with the zone named in the sub-header ("Sept 1–30 · Central Time"). GOAL exports hours in Pacific time; never print an unconverted hour.
- Describe schedule updates by direction and hours: "We increased bids from 8 AM to 11 AM." Never the percentage, the slider value, or an effective bid.
- Contact rate by hour uses Leads as the denominator, shown only for hours or blocks with 30 or more leads.
- Hour ranges read "8 AM to 11 AM", never with an en or em dash between the times.

## No selling to active accounts
These reports go to clients who already bought. Leave out: any "Why GOAL is different" section, a "% exclusive" KPI card, "exclusive, never resold, they chose you" framing, vendor-comparison persuasion, and aspirational close-rate ranges ("2–3% → 8–15%"). Add persuasive or exclusivity framing only when the user explicitly asks (a win-back or prospect pitch).

## Before and after examples

| Banned | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Jordan, your Home line is the workhorse!" | "Home produced 41 of 59 leads (69%)." |
| Sub-header: "Home carries volume; Auto is being refined" | Sub-header: "June 1–30" |
| Badge: "100% Exclusive Leads" | Badge: "Home (CA)" |
| Tag: "Strongest Line", "High Quality" | Tag: "Primary", "Active", "Refining" |
| "Contact rates fall sharply after the first few minutes." | "Metric behind it: contact rate is 31% (18 of 59 leads)." |
| "The ad is doing its job; the constraint is visibility." | "Shoppers clicked the ad on 4.6% of impressions (82 of 1,782)." |
| "Average cost per lead: $17" | "CPA: $334 (3 policies)" |
| "We expect volume to double next month." | "We increased the Home daily budget from $50 to $95." |
| "We raised the 8 AM to 11 AM schedule to 125%." | "We increased bids from 8 AM to 11 AM." |
| "Most leads arrive at 7 AM." (Pacific hour, agent in Eastern) | "39% of leads arrived in the 10 AM hour (Eastern Time)." |
| "Mornings are your best time to call." | "Metric behind it: contact rate 34% (41 of 120 leads) from 8 AM to 11 AM." |
